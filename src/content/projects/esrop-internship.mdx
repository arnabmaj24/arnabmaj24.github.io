## The problem

An IMU's accelerometer is a good gravity reference when the robot is still and a bad one when it's accelerating, because it can't tell gravity from motion. A standard EKF trusts it the same amount either way, and the orientation estimate suffers in hard maneuvers.

## What I built

An adaptive EKF that scales R, the accelerometer's measurement-noise covariance, with measured acceleration. When the robot accelerates hard, the filter trusts the accelerometer less and the gyroscope more; when it's calm, it leans on the accelerometer to correct gyro drift. Orientation is a quaternion, which avoids the singularities you get with Euler angles.

## Testing

I designed three maneuvering tests to compare the adaptive filter with a standard EKF, and ran the same comparison in Simulink and on a mobile robot.

## Result

Over a 10-minute dead-reckoning window on the mobile robot, the adaptive EKF drifted 32% less than the standard one. The work went into a pose-estimation tutorial for the lab.
