## The problem

Our drone talks to the ground station over a directional antenna. During fast flybys the antenna couldn't keep up, the link dropped, and PX4's link-loss failsafe sent the drone back to base more than four times an hour. Every return cut a test flight short.

## What I built

A 2-axis turret (360° of yaw, 180° of pitch) that carries the antenna and points it at the drone. The turret's own position is fixed and entered by hand. The drone's GPS position from PX4 comes in as telemetry over ROS, and the turret's ESP32 runs micro-ROS so it can subscribe to it directly.

From the two positions, the firmware works out the yaw and pitch angles (the inverse kinematics of a pan-tilt) and drives both axes with a PID loop using quaternion math. The servos have encoders, and those gave a more reliable pointing estimate than the turret's IMU, especially in yaw, so the loop runs on encoder feedback. The servo drivers were bought, not built.

## Aiming ahead

Pointing at where the drone *was* isn't good enough: by the time the telemetry arrives and the servos slew, it has moved. So the firmware extrapolates the drone's path from its velocity and recent trajectory in the telemetry and aims at where it's about to be.

Before it went near the field, I simulated the turret tracking the drone in Gazebo, using a URDF model of the turret.

## Result

Failsafe returns from link dropouts went from more than 4 an hour to about 1 every 2 hours: 8× fewer.
