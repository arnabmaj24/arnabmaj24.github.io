## The problem

Fin-stabilized model rockets are simple, but they can't correct for a disturbance once they're off the pad. Three of us set out to build one that steers actively by gimballing its motor.

## My part

The mechanical side was mine: the rocket body and the gimbal that moves the motor. I went through several gimbal designs in Onshape, working against the requirements the team set for gimbal range, actuator mounting and mass. I also built a 3-DOF Simulink model of the rocket with the Aerospace Blockset, and worked with the team on the guidance stack and its tuning instead of handing over a mechanism and walking away.

## How it works

An Unscented Kalman Filter fuses IMU and magnetometer data into an attitude estimate, and a PID attitude controller commands the gimbal to correct it.

## Hardest problem

Fitting a gimbal with enough range and actuator authority into the mass and space budget of a model rocket, without adding so much weight that it works against the flight it's supposed to be correcting. Designing a mechanism around someone else's control requirements is a different job from mechanical design on its own: gimbal range and actuator speed are control requirements, not just mechanical specs.

## Where it stands

It has been bench hot-fire tested but hasn't flown. In the hot-fire tests the attitude oscillations decayed, but they hadn't fully settled by the time the motor burned out, so tuning continues.
